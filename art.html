Rave Central – Artist Directory

A curated selection of classic and contemporary artists whose visuals shaped – and still shape – rave culture.

PEZ – Artwork Icon

Pez (Peter Thorpe)

Cartoon rave characters · Fantazia · Neon chaos

Pez defined the look of early UK rave flyers with hyper-colour characters, playful chaos and iconic Fantazia artwork. His style became the visual language of the early rave explosion.

Official site
Junior Tomlin – Artwork Icon

Junior Tomlin

Airbrushed sci‑fi · Chrome futurism · Fantazia

Often called the “Michelangelo of rave flyers”, Junior Tomlin’s airbrushed sci‑fi landscapes and chrome futurism gave Fantazia and other events their legendary cosmic identity.

Official site
Dan Pearce – Artwork Icon

Dan Pearce

Modern pop‑art · Neon club energy

A leading contemporary pop‑artist whose work often channels club and rave energy. Collected by celebrities and represented by major galleries, his visuals bridge street culture and fine art.

Official site Clarendon Fine Art
Acer‑One – Artwork Icon

Acer‑One

Graffiti‑infused hardcore · Jungle flyers

Acer‑One’s graffiti‑driven style brought raw street energy to hardcore and jungle flyers, becoming a cult favourite among ravers and collectors of underground artwork.

Instagram
Goldie – Artwork Icon

Goldie

Graffiti origins · Metalheadz iconography

Before becoming a jungle legend, Goldie was a graffiti artist whose visuals helped shape the early look of the scene. His Metalheadz iconography remains instantly recognisable worldwide.

Official site
Jonny Martin – Artwork Icon

Jonny Martin

Grassroots rave flyers · Underground visuals

Jonny Martin is known for authentic, underground rave artwork that captures the DIY spirit of the UK party scene, from flyers to posters and bespoke illustrations.

Instagram
Rebecca Try – Artwork Icon

Rebecca Try

Neon‑line illustration · Rave flyers

One of the few prominent female illustrators in the rave‑flyer world, Rebecca Try’s neon‑line style and bold compositions stand out in the visual history of rave culture.

Instagram
Dave Little – Artwork Icon

Dave Little

Acid‑house pioneer · Spectrum · Shoom

A true pioneer of UK club graphics, Dave Little’s work for Spectrum, Shoom and other early acid‑house nights helped define the visual birth of the rave era.

Official site
Floating BSTRD – Artwork Icon

Floating BSTRD

Surreal modern rave‑flyer illustration

Floating BSTRD’s bold, surreal and psychedelic visuals have made him one of the most recognisable contemporary rave‑flyer artists, blending modern design with classic rave energy.

Official site
Alex Solman – Artwork Icon

Alex Solman

Minimalist line‑art · Golden Pudel Club

Known for his distinctive minimalist line‑art, Alex Solman’s work for Golden Pudel Club and beyond has earned him a cult following in techno and experimental club circles.

Official site
Ryandesign_ – Artwork Icon

Ryandesign_ (Ryan Marinello)

Retro‑futurist rave flyers · Scottish scene

Ryandesign_ keeps the classic rave aesthetic alive with modern tools, creating retro‑futurist flyers and visuals for the Scottish rave and club scene.

Instagram
Caterina Bianchini – Artwork Icon

Caterina Bianchini

Bold club‑culture graphics · London

A leading London‑based designer whose bold, expressive graphics have become a staple of modern nightlife and electronic‑music visual culture.

Official site
Courteney Frisby – Photo Icon

Courteney Frisby

Psychedelic nightlife photography

Courteney Frisby captures rave and nightlife with colour‑rich, surreal imagery that feels as intense and immersive as the dancefloor itself.

Instagram
Molly Macindoe – Photo Icon

Molly Macindoe

Free‑party documentary photography

Molly Macindoe’s long‑term documentation of the UK free‑party scene, including her book “Out of Order”, makes her one of the key visual historians of rave culture.

Official site
Dave Swindells – Photo Icon

Dave Swindells

Iconic 90s club and rave photography

As one of the main photographers documenting the birth of rave and club culture, Dave Swindells’ images are essential to the visual memory of the scene.

Official site
Mark “Choci” Williams – Artwork Icon

Mark “Choci” Williams

Rave artwork · Choci’s Chewns

A cult figure whose artwork for Choci’s Chewns and London parties helped define a particular slice of the underground rave aesthetic in the 90s.

The Designers Republic – Artwork Icon

The Designers Republic

Warp Records · Techno futurism

Not strictly rave, but hugely influential in electronic‑music visual culture. Their cyber‑graphic futurism for Warp Records and beyond shaped how techno and club culture looks.

Official site
Joe Rush – Sculpture Icon

Joe Rush (Mutoid Waste Company)

Industrial rave sculptures · Mutant vehicles

Joe Rush and Mutoid Waste Company brought large‑scale industrial sculpture and mutant vehicles to free‑parties and festivals, defining the look of many outdoor rave environments.

Banksy – Artwork Icon

Banksy (early era)

Stencil graffiti · Bristol free‑party roots

While now a global art phenomenon, Banksy’s early stencil work is closely tied to Bristol’s underground and free‑party culture, making him culturally adjacent to rave visuals.

Vinca Petersen – Photo Icon

Vinca Petersen

Travelling sound systems · No System

Vinca Petersen’s book “No System” is one of the most important visual documents of travelling sound systems and 90s rave travellers, capturing the lifestyle around the music.

Official site
Dave Nodz – Suburban Base Icon

Dave Nodz

Suburban Base Records · Hardcore/jungle identity

Dave Nodz was the primary visual architect behind Suburban Base Records, one of the most influential labels in early hardcore and jungle. His bold, comic‑style characters, graffiti‑infused layouts and explosive colour schemes defined the label’s iconic look and became a cornerstone of rave‑art history.
